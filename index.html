Merge files into one PDF

Combine PDFs, photos, and text files into a single document. Drag files to reorder them — the PDF follows the order below.

    Tip: drag rows to reorder, or use the arrow buttons.
    Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files can't be converted inside a browser — export them to PDF first, then drop them here. Unsupported files are simply skipped.

    How to merge files into one PDF

    The whole process happens in this tab. Nothing is queued, nothing is uploaded, and there is no waiting room between you and the result.

    1. Add your files

      Drag them onto the drop area above or click browse to pick them from your device. You can add PDFs, photos, and plain-text files in the same batch, and you can keep adding more after the first drop.

    2. Put them in the right order

      Every file appears as a row. Drag a row up or down, or use the arrow buttons, until the list reads top-to-bottom in the order you want the finished document to read. Files are sorted naturally on arrival, so scan-2.jpg lands before scan-10.jpg instead of after it.

    3. Decide how photos should sit on the page

      Turn on Rotate landscape photos to portrait if you photographed documents sideways and want every page upright. Leave it off when you are combining genuine landscape images such as screenshots or diagrams.

    4. Merge and download

      Press Merge files. The progress bar reports each file as it is processed, then a download button appears. The file is generated in memory and handed straight to your browser's downloads folder.

    Why Office files are skipped

    Rendering a Word or Excel file faithfully means resolving fonts, styles, tracked changes, embedded charts, and pagination rules — the work that a full office suite does. No browser ships with that engine, and any website that claims to convert your .docx is sending the file to a server to do it there. That would break the one promise this site makes. Export to PDF from your own application first (File → Save as → PDF, or File → Export), then drop the PDF here.

    When merging locally is worth the detour

    Most people reach for a PDF merger a few times a year, usually under mild time pressure and usually with documents they would rather not hand to a stranger. A few situations where staying on-device matters more than usual:

    • Applications and paperwork. Rental applications, visa files, and loan submissions typically ask for one PDF containing your ID, payslips, and bank statements — a package that identifies you completely.
    • Medical records. Test results and referral letters are special-category data under the GDPR. Uploading them to an unknown processor is a decision worth avoiding when you do not have to make it.
    • Client and employer work. Many contracts and internal policies forbid sending company documents to third-party services. A tool that never transmits the file sidesteps the question entirely.
    • Bad or metered connections. Merging forty photos of receipts is instant on your own machine and painful over a hotel Wi-Fi upload.

    Ordering, rotation, and page size

    Three behaviours are worth knowing before you merge something important:

    • Existing PDF pages keep their own dimensions. If you merge an A4 report with a US Letter invoice, the output contains both sizes. That is deliberate — resizing would either crop content or add margins.
    • Images are placed on a page sized to fit them, scaled down when they are larger than a standard page so nothing is cut off.
    • Text files use a fixed monospaced layout with generous margins. Long lines wrap; they are never truncated.

    If page sizes need to match exactly — some submission portals insist on it — print each source to A4 first, then merge.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my files really private?

    Yes. Everything runs in JavaScript inside your browser tab. Your files are read into memory on your own device, processed there, and never uploaded, stored, or transmitted anywhere. Close the tab and they are gone. You can load this page, disconnect from the internet, and it still works.

    Is there a file size or page limit?

    There is no hard limit, but processing uses your device's memory. A few hundred pages or a few hundred megabytes is fine on a modern laptop; a ten-year-old phone will struggle sooner. If a very large merge stalls, split it into two batches and merge the two results.

    Can I merge Word or Excel files?

    Not directly. Faithfully converting Office documents requires a rendering engine that browsers do not have, and every site that offers it is doing the conversion on a server. Export to PDF first (File → Save as → PDF), then drop the PDF here alongside your other files.

    Does merging reduce the quality of my pages?

    No. Pages from existing PDFs are copied as objects, not re-rendered, so text stays sharp and selectable and images keep their original encoding. JPEG and PNG files are embedded without re-compression.

    Can I remove or reorder pages inside one of the PDFs?

    Not in this tool — it works at file level, not page level. Extract or delete the pages you do not want in a PDF reader first, save that as its own file, and then merge.

    What happens to passwords, forms, and signatures?

    Password-protected files usually cannot be read at all and will fail with an error; remove the password first. Interactive form fields and digital signatures do not survive a merge, because the merged document is a new file — a signature that covered the old document would no longer be valid. Flatten forms before merging if the filled-in values matter.

    Does the tool work offline?

    Once the page and its two libraries have loaded, yes. You can switch off your connection and keep merging. It is the simplest way to verify the privacy claim for yourself.
